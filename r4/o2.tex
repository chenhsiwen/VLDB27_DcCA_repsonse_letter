\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]

\item \textit{W2 (part): ``The practical instantiation and validation of $\omega$ are underexplained, especially under complex constraints.''}
\end{itemize}
\smallskip

\noindent\textbf{Response.}
We add concrete $\omega$ instantiations and describe its lifecycle. Building on the loan example in Section~2.4 (where \emph{``$\text{Age}\neq 28$ is infeasible''}), tabular $\omega$ is a weighted sum of per-column monotonic edit costs with $\infty$ for immutable attributes; temporal $\omega$ combines an alignment cost~\cite{senin2008dynamic} with a dynamics-validity predicate; text/image $\omega$ uses a representation-space cost with a semantic admissibility predicate (Table~\ref{tab:modality}). For complex constraints, the property that \emph{``infeasible transformations are assigned infinite cost''} lets composite constraints be expressed as a conjunction whose violation drives $\omega\to\infty$, keeping admissibility and cost well defined. Our validity study (Table~\ref{tab:validity}) instantiates such an $\omega$ (categorical immutable attributes) and shows DcCA respects it whenever evidence exists.

\noindent\emph{Revision to Section~2 (new paragraph on $\omega$ instantiation):}
\begin{quote}
\textcolor{blue}{In practice, $\omega$ is instantiated per modality and deployment. For tabular data, it is a weighted sum of per-column monotonic edit costs with $\infty$ assigned to immutable attributes (e.g., age); for temporal data, it combines an alignment cost with a dynamics-validity predicate; for text and image, it combines a representation-space cost with a semantic-admissibility predicate (Section~4). Operationally, $\omega$ is \emph{defined} from domain or policy rules, optionally \emph{learned} from logged interventions or expert preferences, and \emph{validated} against held-out feasible outcomes or expert audit. Composite constraints are conjunctive, so violating any constraint yields $\omega=\infty$, cleanly separating feasibility from intervention effort.}
\end{quote}
